Overall

Overall performance

I think my agents did a good job. The way they solved the tickets was logical, and they deferred to human judgment when necessary. I am satisfied with the way the agents executed and do not think they made superfluous calls.

How actuals compared to expected

  • Ticket 101: The actual outcome matched the expected outcome for the most part. The key difference is that it did not call one predicted tool (prepare_payment). Also, when building this system, my AI added a safety feature that does not allow an agent to delegate to itself, so when the Boss tried to call itself, that call was blocked.
  • Ticket 102: The actuals matched the expected exactly.
  • Ticket 103: The differences were in the tools called (check_invoices was not called). The inventory agent was also called twice, and the order the agents were called in differed slightly. Additionally, there was a cash conflict when approving the orders. I was having Claude do the approvals, and Claude flagged this to me, so I told it not to proceed with going into the red, which changed the outcome.

What would have been simpler

All of these tickets would have been simpler to resolve with one agent with tools. All of these calls happened in sequence and nothing happened concurrently, so there was no reason for the multi-agent team. Running the calls in sequence also slows the system down, but it is a safety measure, since only one ticket runs at a time and two runs can't spend the same cash. This could be changed in future iterations. The Boss is particularly ineffective, since its sole purpose is delegation, which would be unnecessary if we had one agent.

Three new problems my agents could solve

  1. If a vendor asked about an overdue purchase order, my agents could solve this problem using almost solely the tools in Accounting.
  2. If a customer asked where the warehouses are, my agents could address this by invoking both the Facilities agent and the Customer Service agent. (It is questionable whether we would want to release this information, but I believe my agents would.)
  3. With the existing tools, my agents could also be programmed to automatically order new stock when inventory gets low.

Three new problems my agents could not solve

  1. My agents would not be able to respond to customer complaints about product quality, for example if the Yale logo on a Campus Customs hoodie was faded.
  2. My agents would not, at this time, be able to prepare Campus Customs accounting statements (cash flows, balance sheets, etc.).
  3. My agents could also not track the shipping status of customer orders, which is something our campus customers would likely really want.
